1

Keep the arrow column on the left

KeepOne arrow column gives every row the same name space and makes the board scan as a list: arrow, trail ID, name. Ski areas use the same order of name, difficulty symbol and arrow on trail signs, with the same color and shape system across every resort.

2

Move the Green Line badge under the name

ChangeBeside the name, the badge costs about 5″ of the 15.4″. Under the name it costs nothing. A 1.5″ badge under a 1.72″ cap fits in the 4″ row with room to spare. This keeps 2″ names, fits Outfitter Trail on every board, and leaves the badge reading “GREEN LINE” as the Playbook requires.

3

Drop the “To” prefix on boards

ChangeOn a board, the arrow already says “this way to”. Dropping “To” saves about 1.2″ per row and makes every row read the same. Keep “To” only in written directions, such as the inventory and the website.

4

Add a “Sign Name” column to the trail inventory

ChangeFixing long names in the data keeps the type size fixed. Rule: 16 characters max at 2″, used on every sign type, map and app. The full name stays in the inventory. These 13 need one:

  • Diamondback - beginning section
  • Western Diamondback
  • Southern Diamondback
  • Eastern Diamondback
  • Diamondback easy out
  • Diamondback easy out lower
  • Diamondback easy out upper
  • Diamondback Ledges
  • Powerline Easement
  • Brokefoot viewing trail
  • Charlie's to Lucky's
  • Kicking and Screaming
  • Road to Primitive Camping

Green Light Cut Through also needs one once it's added to the inventory.

5

Three board sizes, all 24″ wide

ChangeFewer sizes mean fewer templates, simpler reorders and spares that fit anywhere. Every height divides 96″, so a 4′ × 8′ sheet cuts with no waste.

4″ brandmark header + 4″ per row. More than 5 rows means a second board on the same post, or a simpler junction.

6

Don't make one-row boards

ChangeA board with one trail and one arrow (Green Line Signs 02, 10, 12 and 14) is a trail marker or route marker doing a board's job. Use the 3.5″ marker decals or a small route-marker plaque instead. These are cheaper, they're already a standard part, and they keep boards for real decision points.

7

Print rows as replaceable strips (optional)

ConsiderPrint each 4″ row as its own 24″ × 4″ strip on a blank board with the brandmark. When a trail is renamed or re-rated, you reprint one strip, not the board. Strips come from one template, and 24 fit on a sheet. The tradeoff is more mounting hardware per board.

8

Material and print spec

ChangeAsk the printer for UV-cured ink plus a UV laminate. Standard PVC can turn brittle or fade under long, intense sun, and a UV laminate improves its outdoor performance. Plan PVC as a replaceable layer: typical outdoor PVC life is 3–5 years. Aluminum composite (ACM) is the upgrade for the sunniest or most important junctions. The same art works on either, so the templates don't change.

  • No bleed: the inset frame hides small cutting errors.
  • Same hole pattern on every board, so any board fits any post.
  • One face per direction of approach. Don't print arrows on the back of a board.
  • Sign ID printed in the frame, matched to a GIS point, for reorders and replacements.
